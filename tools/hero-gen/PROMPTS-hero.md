# ThirstTrap hero scene prompts

Concept art for the landing page hero, generated by driving the ChatGPT website
in a signed-in browser, one scene per fresh chat, with reference images
attached. These are the shipping plates: the page renders them directly, encoded
by `encode.py`.

    /home/dheirav/Code/Axl/tools/chatgpt-gen/browser.sh    # its own terminal, leave open
    tools/hero-gen/regen.sh                                # all fourteen
    /home/dheirav/Code/Axl/tools/chatgpt-gen/progress.sh --watch

Run it from a terminal you can type into. When ChatGPT changes its page the
script stops and asks you to do that one step by hand.

Use `regen.sh` rather than calling `gen.mjs` directly. The direct invocation is
wrong in three ways that all fail quietly:

  gen.mjs resolves --prompts and --out with path.join against its own root, and
  path.join does not treat an absolute second argument as absolute, it
  concatenates, so an absolute path becomes mascot-source/home/dheirav/...

  it has to run from the chatgpt-gen directory, and progress.sh lives there too

  it never overwrites, so with ledger.png already in out/ the new plate lands as
  ledger-2.png, which check-plates.py skips and encode.py publishes as a file no
  page loads. The run looks like it worked and changes nothing. regen.sh moves
  the current set to out/prev-<timestamp>/ first.

Then score the new plates against the room before encoding anything:

    python3 tools/hero-gen/check-plates.py --src
    cd tools/hero-gen && python3 encode.py --harmonise

## Why this file was rewritten

The first version of these prompts described a look and never described a place.
The style block fixed the palette and the flat-vector treatment, and then each
scene invented its own setting: "a windowsill" in one, "a wooden table" in the
next, "a dark wall beside a window" in a third. Nothing told the generator that
the sill in `finger-test` is the same sill as in `shelf-evening`, or where the
lamp hangs, so it answered differently every time.

Measured on the fourteen shipped plates, by the horizontal centroid of the
brightest tenth of each picture, which is where the light actually lands:

  the lamp sits left in five shots and right in two, a swing of 0.56
  three beats light the same moment from opposite sides in their two orientations
  foliage came back at hue 60 to 102 against a spec of 134, olive rather than green
  terracotta came back 12 to 19 degrees too orange, though consistently so

The terracotta drift does not break continuity because every plate is wrong in
the same direction, so the pots still read as the same pots. The lamp does break
it, and it is the thing you notice: scrolling from `roots` to `scale-table`, the
light jumps across the room.

The fix is that the room is now written down, and it is not invented here. It
comes from `tools/blender/world.py`, which models the room once and puts a
camera in it for every beat, so it cannot disagree with itself.
`tools/blender/continuity.py` projects the lamp into each of those cameras and
writes `continuity.json`, and the per-shot lines below are read off it. The
measured layout, in metres, floor at z = 0, +x to the right as you face the
window:

    window wall   y = +1.06, glazed from z 0.95 to 2.05
    sill          top at z 0.95, four pots along it at x -0.52, -0.21, +0.10, +0.41
    table         centred (0.42, 0.06), top at z 0.755
    lamp          (0.60, 0.24, 1.26), over the table, at the right-hand end
    ledger        pinned to the pier at x -1.02, on the left of the window

## Attachments, every scene

`../../../ThirstTrap/tools/hero-gen/refs/ref-palette.png` goes on every prompt. The hex codes in the style block
were ignored on all fourteen plates, so the palette is now a picture as well as
a list.

`../../../ThirstTrap/tools/hero-gen/refs/layout-<scene>.png` goes on the scene it is named for. That is a render of
the real room from that beat's camera, brightened so the geometry reads, and it
is the attachment that does the work: the generator is placing things in a room
it can see rather than one it has to imagine. It is a layout reference only. Its
colour and its lighting are both wrong on purpose, and the style block overrides
them.

## Style block (paste first, every time)

    Flat vector illustration: no outlines anywhere, form separated by steps of
    value alone, at most three flat values per object, hard-edged polygonal
    shadows rather than gradients, and a fine film grain over everything.
    Gradients are permitted only for emitted light such as a lamp glow.

    Use ONLY the palette in the attached palette card and ignore the colours in
    every other attachment. Ground and shadow: very dark warm brown, #1E1913 to
    #2E2820. Paper and text: #F5F0E2. Foliage: #2F5D3A in shadow and #43794F in
    light. These are GREEN. Do not render the leaves olive, yellow-green or
    khaki; if a leaf reads closer to brown than to green it is wrong.
    Terracotta: a muted #8C4F43, less saturated than the accent and distinctly
    red rather than orange. Night sky through a window: #22303C. A single warm
    accent, #C98A3A, covering well under one percent of the picture and marking
    exactly one thing.

    A quiet domestic interior at night. Calm, observational, unsentimental. No
    text, no labels, no logos, no user interface, no watermark. Nobody is
    smiling at the camera.

    THE ROOM. Every scene in this set is the same room, seen from a different
    place in it, and they will be shown one after another. Treat the following
    as fact.

    The room has one window in the far wall, running most of its width, glazed
    from about waist height to above head height, with a night city and a moon
    beyond it. A deep sill runs along the bottom of that window and four small
    terracotta pots stand on it in a row. To the left of the window is a narrow
    strip of wall, and a sheet of cream paper is pinned to it. In front of the
    window and over to the right is a wooden table.

    There is exactly one lamp. It hangs low over the right-hand end of that
    table, and it is the only warm light in the room. Everything else is
    moonlight through the window, which is cold and much weaker. The lamp never
    moves between scenes. Each scene below states where it falls for that
    camera, and that statement wins over anything the attached layout image
    seems to show.

    THE PROPS. These objects appear in more than one scene and must be the SAME
    object each time, not merely the same kind of object. Draw them exactly as
    described here and do not redesign them to suit a composition.

    PHONE: one slim flat slab. Body #22262B, a neutral near-black charcoal, NOT
    navy and NOT blue. Around its edge, and clearly visible on every edge in
    every scene, a bright pale metal band the colour of #B9BCC0, about as thick
    as the phone's own depth. Rounded corners, no case, no buttons visible from
    above. Its screen is on in some scenes and off in others and each scene says
    which, but the body colour and that bright band never change. If the band
    does not read as bright metal against the dark body, it is wrong.

    MUG: plain cream stoneware, straight-sided, about as tall as it is wide,
    with a solid D-shaped handle that is ALWAYS visible and always drawn. Half
    full of dark coffee. It is a mug with a handle, never a tumbler, never a
    beaker, never a lidded cup.

    SCALE: a flat square kitchen scale, white, low and slab-like with no bowl,
    and a small dark rectangular readout window set into its front lip.

    WATERING CAN: slim galvanised metal, pale cool grey, with a long thin
    straight spout running up from low on the body and a single high arched
    handle over the top. Not plastic, not coloured, not a bulbous or rounded
    can, no rose or sprinkler head on the spout, no decoration.

    POTS: EVERY terracotta pot in every scene is the same design, including the
    one standing on the scale and the one cut in half. A plain straight-walled
    pot whose sides slope evenly inward to a narrower base, with one flat rim
    lip at the top and nothing else. No belly, no bulge, no urn or bowl shape,
    no flared bucket mouth, no banding, no pattern, no saucer. Same muted clay
    colour throughout. The four on the sill are this pot at four slightly
    different sizes.

    PLANTS: every plant in this set is the same kind of plant. Six to ten broad
    leaves, each a rounded oval coming to a soft point, on short upright stalks
    rising straight out of the soil in a loose rosette. No trailing vines, no
    branching woody stems, no narrow strap or spear leaves, no spikes or
    rosettes of the agave kind, no variegation, no flowers. The four on the sill
    are this plant at four slightly different sizes, and the one on the scale is
    the second pot from the left, carried over to the table, so it is that same
    plant in that same pot.

    BOOKS: a short stack of three or four hardbacks lying flat at the left end
    of the sill, dark cloth covers, no titles.

    LEDGER SHEET: one sheet of cream paper, portrait, held by a single brass pin
    at its top centre, ruled into seven evenly spaced rows by fine hairlines
    with a heavier rule under the heading. Not a notebook, not a clipboard, not
    a card, not taped or framed, no curl, and never more than one sheet.

## Portrait versions

Same seven beats in nine by sixteen. These are drawn portrait rather than cropped
from the landscape ones, which is what makes them work on a phone: a 9:16 source
into a 9:19.5 viewport keeps 82% of its width, where cropping the 16:9 kept 26%
and upscaled it four times over.

The bodies below are the landscape bodies, copied, plus a tail for the tall
frame. The lamp sentence and the prop list in particular are copied rather than
rewritten, because both are things that drifted when they were written twice.
