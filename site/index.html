ThirstTrap

A plant diary that weighs the pot. Water has weight, so a pot can tell you when it actually needs watering, instead of you watering everything on the same evening.

The arithmetic

Why weighing works

Water is most of what a pot loses between waterings. Soil does not evaporate; roots and leaves move water out, and that shows up as grams. Weigh the pot just after watering and you have the full mark. Weigh it again a few times and the slope between those points is the drying rate, in grams a day, for that pot in that room in this week's weather.

The prediction is then division: how far above the trigger weight it is, divided by how fast it is falling. No plant database, no averages from somebody else's windowsill.

Measured, not assumed

It says when it does not know

There are seven things the app will say instead of a date: too few readings, no measurable drying yet, a pot that has just been repotted, a plant that has gone. Each one explains itself and says what would fix it.

An app that names a confident wrong day teaches watering by the calendar, which is the habit this exists to replace. Honest uncertainty is the product, not a caveat.

Anti-goals

No streaks. No counts of what you missed.
No tone of voice about it.

A missed day is not a failure, it is a day when nothing needed doing. The app is laid out like an almanac rather than a habit tracker: rules and dated entries, not cards that can be full or empty. A blank line in an almanac is not a reproach.

Offline-first, and it means it

Nothing leaves the phone

No account, no sync, no server. The diary is a database on your device and a zip you keep. One setting can look a plant name up online, it is off by default, and it sends the name and nothing else.

Built in the open

Kotlin, Compose, Room

Four modules, a pure-JVM domain layer holding the watering model, and 277 tests over it. The model is written down in docs/WATERING-MODEL.md before it was written in code, including the parts that refuse to answer.

Your pots already know.
They just need somebody to write it down.
